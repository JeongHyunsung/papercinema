## 4. Deliberate wording choices (accuracy over hype)

- **"2.53× lower sequencing overhead"**, never a generic "2.53× faster". The metric (T_setup + T_CK→Q, 0.9 V) is named on screen, and the race shows durations, not a fabricated setup / clock-to-Q split.
- **FF1's energy cost is shown right after the climax** (2.26 vs 1.53 fJ).
- **The race shows only FF1 vs. TGFF** (the paper's headline comparison), both labelled as *circuits* with their transistor counts, separate from the FSM shown just before. The scatter that immediately follows shows all 18 Table 2 flip-flops, including the SeGen variant at 19.2 ps.
- **No "7 orders of magnitude"** (Table 1 marks the 3-bit speedup N/A).
- **No reliability superiority claim** (FF1's race immunity is below TGFF's in Table 2).
- **FF20 is not presented as a discovery** (§4.5.3).
- **The step-5 (conflict) caption says "the candidate path 01 → 11 → 10 must leave (11, 11)"**, scoping the contradiction to that branch, which is all that is pruned.
- **No conceptual visuals remain.** v3 removed the enumerate-vs-validate trees (conceptual Fig. 3 sketch); every graph on screen is computed data. The 468 cells' positions inside the square and the 20 amber cells' positions in the block carry no meaning (unordered unit charts; the block is shuffled so it implies no C_FF ordering).
- **Removed earlier:** the decorative 3-bit particle field and the field of cubes before the title (v1), and the conceptual trees (v2).

## 5. Discrepancies found while verifying (for the authors)

- **C_FF of FF4 and FF5.** Recomputing C_FF = literals + product terms of the exact minimum SOP from the Fig. 8f tables gives ≤ 20 for 18 of the 20 FFs, but **FF4 = 29 and FF5 = 22**, which would exclude them under §4.2's C_FF ≤ 20. Possible causes: a transcription of Fig. 8f, a different literal-count convention, or different don't-care handling. The film shows no per-FF C_FF values.
- **3-bit initial input** is not stated; the replay assumes (D, CK) = (0, 1) as in the 2-bit example. FF1 is stable at ((0,1), 011) under its own table, so the choice is consistent.
